🪟 Our Materials
Our Four Material Packages
We don't sell one option and hope it fits — we present four packages and let the customer choose. This isn't about having more options. It's about removing confusion and building trust. When people feel ownership, they say yes.
Premium
Premium Package
The most luxurious option on the market
Stone or Specialty Panels
For customers remodeling a forever home or creating a showpiece. Not for everyone — but the right customer will know immediately.
"This is for people who are remodeling for design. It's about look, feel, and experience."
Performance
Performance Package
The most widely used material on the market
Acrylic or PVC
Durable, smooth, and nonporous. What most people imagine when they think of a remodeled bath. Minimal maintenance without stretching the budget.
"This is what a lot of families choose — it's familiar and proven."
⭐ Most Popular
Smart Choice Package
Our niche in the market
Composite + ShowerShield
Our most popular system — but we never call it that upfront. Upgraded wall panels with exclusive foam core backing. Feels luxury without the luxury price.
"This is the one that balances everything — comfort, durability, and beauty."
Standard
Standard Package
The simplest, most affordable option
Fiberglass
Simple, functional, affordable. Best for landlords, flips, and pure budget shoppers. Don't disrespect this tier — for the right customer, it's a smart move.
"This one gets the job done. It's clean, dependable, and easy to maintain."

Field Notes — Presenting Without Pressure
How we guide, not close
How to Present Four Packages
  • Introduce the idea of "options" early — even before price comes up
  • Describe packages in human terms: talk about the kind of buyer, not just the specs
  • Show all four visually, calmly — no dramatic reveal
  • Stay neutral. Don't recommend anything until the customer reacts
  • Let them sit with it. Silence is powerful
  • Only show pricing after curiosity is built — then go silent again
Guardrails — What Not to Do
  • Don't skip a package because you assume what they can afford
  • Don't write packages down by hand — show them visually with pricing software
  • Don't say "We usually recommend this one" — let the Smart Choice speak for itself
  • Don't ask "Which one do you want?" immediately after showing prices
⚠️ When you limit options for the customer — even with good intentions — you remove their agency. And when people lose agency, they say no.
"When the customer says, 'That's the one I want,' before you do — you've done it right."
